Degree days measure how cold it was: each day adds how far its average fell below 15.5 °C. kWh per degree day lets one winter be compared fairly with another, whatever the weather; if it rises, the heat pump (or its settings) is working less efficiently. Winters run October to April; months with little heating are left blank, as hot water would swamp the figure.
Your heat pump's details (for the estimated SCOP)
Cylinder size: the litres on the hot water cylinder's label. With the heat pump's modes (the hot_water_mode_entity and heating_mode_entity options), the tracker measures the heat put into the cylinder at each reheat and uses it to estimate the heat pump's SCOP.
From the heat loss survey done for the heat pump (an MCS heat loss report, say). With it, the tracker estimates the heat your home needed each day from how cold it was, and divides it by the electricity the heat pump used for heating: an estimated SCOP. Surveys tend to overstate heat loss, so treat it as a guide, best for comparing one month or winter with another.
