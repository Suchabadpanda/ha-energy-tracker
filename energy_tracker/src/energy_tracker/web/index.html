Cost by device
Each device pays for the electricity that actually supplied it, half hour by half hour: grid power at that half hour's price, and battery power at what charging the battery cost. So a car charged overnight costs the night rate, and the house run from the battery by day carries the cost of charging it. The standing charge and export credit belong to the house as a whole and are left out. See "Running costs by device" for earlier months. The heat pump's true cost adds what the solar it used (straight away or through the battery) would have earned if exported: solar is free to use, but using it gives up that payment.

      Heat pump and the weather

      PeriodDaysAverage outside Heat pumpDegree dayskWh per degree day
      Degree days measure how cold it was: each day adds how far its average fell below 15.5 °C. kWh per degree day lets one winter be compared fairly with another, whatever the weather; if it rises, the heat pump (or its settings) is working less efficiently. Winters run October to April; months with little heating are left blank, as hot water would swamp the figure.

      Return on investment

      Enter what the system cost below to see how much of it has been paid back and when it should break even.
      System cost and settings
      Savings are what your household's electricity would have cost with no solar or battery, less what you actually paid. By default the comparison uses your own tariff, so cheap-rate charging of a car still counts as cheap. To measure against a different tariff, add it under Compare tariffs and choose it here. The three yearly percentages are optional: fill any in to see a second break-even date that allows for them. Leave them empty to keep to today's prices and performance.

      Extra income

      DateForExportedAmount
      Add income or change settings
      For money earned on top of your tariff, such as Axle Energy payments for exporting during grid events. It counts towards Return on investment. Add entries by hand here. If Home Assistant has an Axle event sensor, export events are also recorded automatically, with the payment estimated as the energy exported during the event times the rate below. Use Edit on an entry to replace an estimate with what you were actually paid. Regular income, such as Axle's monthly payments, is carried forward in Return on investment's projection; one-off income, such as a referral bonus, is counted but not projected.

      Bill check

      ImportedCharged ExportedExport payment
      Add a bill
      Enter the figures from a bill or statement to compare them with what the tracker measured over the same dates. Fill in whichever you have, or choose the bill's PDF and press Read to have them filled in for you to check. Choose several PDFs at once to read them all into a list and save them together. PDFs are read on this device and are not kept or sent anywhere. "Charged" is the electricity charge including the standing charge and VAT, before any export payment. The inverter and the supplier's meter are separate instruments, so a difference of a percent or two is normal.

      Notes

      Pin a note to a day, such as "new tariff" or "heat pump serviced", to explain a change in the figures later. Notes show as a yellow marker on the history chart and the return on investment chart; point at the marker to read them.

      Battery used for the plan
      The plan needs to know how much the battery holds and how fast it charges. Both are worked out from your readings; enter your own figures here if they look wrong, or to try a bigger battery. Leave a box empty to go back to the worked-out figure.

      Tariff rates

      FromTariffImportExport Standing charge
      Add or change rates
      For a price change, enter the date the new rates start: earlier days keep their old rates. To correct rates already listed, choose Edit on that row. Enter prices as they appear on your bill before VAT and set VAT to 5, or enter prices that already include VAT and set VAT to 0. VAT is added to import and the standing charge, not to export. The standard rate applies all day unless you add time windows with a different rate: one for a single cheap period, or several. For an Octopus tariff whose price changes every half hour (Agile) or from time to time (a variable tariff), choose it under published prices: each half hour is then charged at the price Octopus published for it, which already includes VAT, and the rates typed here are only used where no price has been published. The standing charge is always the one typed here. On a fixed-price deal, enter the last day the price is guaranteed and the page will remind you a month before it ends.

      Backup and restore

      Save everything you have entered as one file: tariff rates, tariffs to compare, bills, extra income, notes, and the settings for return on investment, the planner and folded sections. Restoring a file replaces all of those with what the file holds. Readings are not in the file: they are kept by Home Assistant's own backups. The file contains your bill figures and costs, so keep it somewhere private.
